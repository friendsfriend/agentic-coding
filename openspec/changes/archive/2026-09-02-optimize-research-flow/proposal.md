## Why

The research→wiki flow has two rough edges in practice. First, after the researcher hands off, the wiki agent does not act on the structured handoff directives it was given — it re-runs broad, open-ended discovery ("scans for a million different things") before it settles on what to document, wasting a full drafting cycle. Second, the research flow's wiki review does not match the standard flows: the developer never gets the same rich line-anchored review popup for the drafted wiki entries, `close-research` lets the developer skip the wiki entirely at any point, and approving the wiki silently auto-closes the workspace. The developer wants the wiki entry to be a mandatory, reviewed step once drafting begins, using the same approval UI the standard flows already provide, with closing gated behind approval.

## What Changes

- **Split the shared `wiki` role into two dedicated wiki agents** instead of one role with a conditional, handoff-detecting prompt: a `wiki` role (unchanged discovery-based drafting for openspec/implementation and wiki-only workflows) and a new `research-wiki` role (directive-first drafting, used only by the research flow's `core.wiki` step). Each role has its own instruction file; a shared base file keeps the source-repository boundary, update-first sequence, and authoring/review rules identical for both. The `research-wiki` role SHALL start by executing exactly the recorded per-concept directives (create/update the named concepts with the listed claims) and limit repository/wiki inspection to targeted corroboration of those directives, rather than performing broad open-ended rediscovery to figure out what to document. Because it is a separate role with its own instructions, this behavior SHALL NOT change the `wiki` role's openspec/implementation `core.wiki` behavior, which has no directives and still relies on discovery.
- Give the research flow the **same wiki review UI as the standard flows**: at `core.wiki-approval` the research flow SHALL open the same line-anchored wiki review popup (drafted-concept list + markdown view + comment/approve/request-changes) that the wiki-only and openspec flows use, instead of the generic action-notice list.
- **Make the wiki step mandatory once the researcher hands off, and gate closing on approval.** After a handoff into `core.wiki`, `close-research` SHALL no longer be offered during `core.wiki` or `core.wiki-approval`; the only forward paths are wiki drafting → approval or requesting changes. Approving the wiki SHALL transition to an explicit completion gate where "Close workspace" is a deliberate developer action, rather than auto-closing on approval. While research is still purely conversational (`core.research`, before any handoff), `close-research` remains available so a Q&A-only session with nothing to document can still be closed.
- Extend the research graph so the successful path becomes `core.research` → `core.wiki` → `core.wiki-approval` → `core.completed` → `core.closed`, reusing the existing `core.completed` close gate the other flows use, while keeping the research graph free of implementation/verification/archive/delivery/PR stages.

### Modified Capabilities
- `research-workflow`: The wiki-stage handoff requirement is strengthened so the dedicated `research-wiki` role acts directive-first on a research handoff (targeted corroboration only, no broad rediscovery); this is now inherent to the distinct role rather than a step/workflow-conditional branch, so it does not affect the `wiki` role's openspec `core.wiki` step. The lifecycle requirement changes to route through a `core.completed` close gate after `core.wiki-approval`. The closure requirement changes so `close-research` is available only while research is purely conversational (no handoff yet) and is removed during `core.wiki`/`core.wiki-approval`; approval no longer auto-closes and closing becomes an explicit action at `core.completed`. A review requirement is added so the research flow's `core.wiki-approval` uses the same line-anchored wiki review popup as the standard flows.
- `knowledge-wiki`: The wiki-authoring-isolation requirement is widened from a single `wiki` role to the two dedicated wiki-writing roles (`wiki` and `research-wiki`), with identical write/promotion restrictions for both.
